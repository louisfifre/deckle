# Voice and tone

Voice is the product's personality, chosen once; tone is how it bends to the moment. The voice chart below is the seam that keeps the patterns product-agnostic: the skeleton stays invariant, the chart renders it.

## Tone

Four independent axes position a product: funny ↔ serious, formal ↔ casual, respectful ↔ irreverent, enthusiastic ↔ matter-of-fact. Extremes are not required — when nothing else decides, casual, conversational, and moderately enthusiastic is the tested sweet spot. Trust outweighs friendliness by far: playfulness in a serious moment costs more than it earns. When personality and clarity conflict, clarity wins.

## Tone by user state

| User state | Tone | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Frustrated — error, blocked | empathetic, directive, the fix first | humor, exclamation, cheer |
| Confused — new, lost | patient, explanatory, literal | jargon, irony |
| Cautious — payment, destructive, privacy | serious, transparent, precise | playfulness, vagueness |
| Confident — power user mid-flow | efficient, direct, minimal | hand-holding |
| Successful — just done | positive, brief; the one slot for delight | over-celebration |

## Apology

An apology earns its place only when the product is at fault or harm occurred — and then it explains: what went wrong, and the way back. The explanation does the work a bare sorry can't, and repetition cheapens it. External conditions get no apology at all — state the fact and the road back: "You're not connected. Let's get you back online."

## Voice chart

One row per product principle; five columns render it into text decisions. The row below is the house voice — reuse it unless the product declares its own:

| Product principle | Vocabulary | Verbosity | Grammar | Punctuation & caps | Ideal example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm and capable | everyday words, no superlatives | minimal — cut until it breaks | verb-first imperatives | sentence case, no exclamation | "Transcript ready. Press Ctrl+V to paste." |

Sharpen the chart with target words — the tone written toward — and anti-tone words — what it must never sound like. The anti-list catches drift the target list misses.

## One pattern, three voices

The same error skeleton rendered through three charts — the pattern holds, the voice moves:

- Plain utility: "Couldn't save the recording. Check that the output folder still exists."
- Warm consumer: "We couldn't save your recording — your audio is safe. Check that the output folder still exists."
- Terse pro tool: "Couldn't save: output folder not found. Choose another folder."

## Person

You is the default, often implied — "Store files online", not "You can store files online". I marks the user's ownership in a control: "Remember my password". We is reserved for accepting fault, and for privacy statements where the maker must be the visible speaker; everywhere else it looms.
