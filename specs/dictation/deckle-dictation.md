# Speak as long as you want, over everything, and get back a well-framed text

## Need

### Context of use

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) thinks aloud, sometimes for more than an hour. They talk about what passes before their eyes, one [subject](GLOSSARY.md#subject) then another, often while doing something else. They are at their computer, or elsewhere with their phone. What they say belongs to no [application](../GLOSSARY.md#application).

### User need

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) needs to start the [listening](GLOSSARY.md#listening) and stop attending to it, in order to get back everything they said in a well-framed text that they put wherever they want.

### Intended outcome

1. One [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture) starts the [listening](GLOSSARY.md#listening) from anywhere, and the same one stops it.
2. While they speak, nothing asks for their attention and nothing rushes them.
3. They are never cut off: the listening goes on through a [hesitation](GLOSSARY.md#hesitation) or a [silence](GLOSSARY.md#silence), however long.
4. Only their [voice](GLOSSARY.md#voice) counts: a rub or a [noise](GLOSSARY.md#noise) around them does not pass for speech.
5. An hour holds up as well as two minutes, and nothing of what they said is lost.
6. They change subject, [pause](GLOSSARY.md#pause), resume: the [change of subject](GLOSSARY.md#change-of-subject) is marked automatically or by one gesture of theirs, and the text keeps these separations.
7. To find their thread again, their last words read at a glance, without hiding what they are looking at.
8. The [transcription](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe) says what they said and what they meant, and nothing they did not say.
9. The experience is the same on the computer and on the phone.

## Journey

| The user wants to | The product must |
| --- | --- |
| start speaking from wherever they are, without leaving what they do | start the [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation) on one [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture), the same across the [system](../GLOSSARY.md#system), and leave them where they were |
| think aloud for as long as it takes, often doing something else | keep [listening](GLOSSARY.md#listening) without asking for anything, without cutting them on a [pause](GLOSSARY.md#pause) or a [hesitation](GLOSSARY.md#hesitation), and take only their [voice](GLOSSARY.md#voice) |
| find their thread again | show their last words at a glance, out of the way of what they are looking at |
| change subject, pause, resume | mark the [change of subject](GLOSSARY.md#change-of-subject) automatically or on one gesture of theirs, and keep these separations in the text |
| stop and use what they said | stop on the same gesture, and have the whole text ready, framed, for them to put where they want |
| choose once how their dictations are [rewritten](../GLOSSARY.md#rewrite) | apply that choice to every dictation, without asking again |

Thinking aloud, finding the thread and changing subject happen together, while the dictation runs.

## Rules

1. [What the product shows is its real state.](../deckle.rules.md)
2. [Everything the product tells the user reaches them with animations off as well.](../deckle.rules.md)
3. [Everything the product tells the user reaches them with colours off as well.](../deckle.rules.md)
4. Whatever stops a [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation), what was said in it is found again, and the [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) says so when it gives it back, and when only part of it came back.
5. [Smoothed](../GLOSSARY.md#smooth) or not, the [transcription](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe) keeps what the [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) said and what they meant.

## Workflow

### Starting

When it goes well:

1. From wherever they are, one [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture) starts a [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation).
2. Nothing is taken from what they were doing: they go on with it.
3. The first words they say after the gesture are in the text: they wait for no signal to speak.
4. The [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) shows them that it [listens](GLOSSARY.md#listening).

Otherwise:

5. Nothing on the machine can [transcribe](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe) speech: no dictation starts, and the product tells them what is missing and where to get it.
6. No [microphone](../GLOSSARY.md#microphone) can be used: no dictation starts, and the product tells them so.

### Speaking

When it goes well:

1. The [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation) goes on until they stop it, however long they are [silent](GLOSSARY.md#silence): nothing counts down, and they never make a sound to keep it going.
2. A [hesitation](GLOSSARY.md#hesitation), a stammer or a silence in the middle of a sentence never splits the sentence in the text.
3. Only their [voice](GLOSSARY.md#voice) adds text: no other sound around them does.
4. Nothing the [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) shows presses them or asks for a [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture).
5. An hour goes as two minutes: the same gestures, nothing slows down.

Otherwise:

6. The product hears no voice from them in the first seconds of the dictation: it tells them once, and the dictation goes on.

### Finding the thread

When it goes well:

1. A glance is enough to read the last words they said: the newest words are always in view, and the older ones make room for them.
2. What shows their words never hides what they are looking at: as they move toward it, what lies under it can be read and used.
3. When their text is [rewritten](../GLOSSARY.md#rewrite) while they speak, what they are reading does not move.

Otherwise:

4. A rewrite fails: they keep the text they were reading.

### Changing subject

When it goes well:

1. They change [subject](GLOSSARY.md#subject), [pause](GLOSSARY.md#pause) and resume without leaving what they are doing.
2. The [change of subject](GLOSSARY.md#change-of-subject) is marked automatically, or by one [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture) of theirs.
3. The text keeps each subject apart.

### Stopping

When it goes well:

1. The same [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture) stops the [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation) at once: the last words said before it are in the text, and nothing said after it is.
2. The whole text, [rewritten](../GLOSSARY.md#rewrite) when they chose it, is ready for them to put where they want, and the [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) tells them so. The product puts it nowhere by itself.

Otherwise:

3. Nothing was said: nothing is made ready, and whatever they had ready to put elsewhere before stays ready.
4. Part of what they said was recorded but could not be [transcribed](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe): the rest is ready, and the product tells them a part is missing.
5. A rewrite fails: they still get their whole text, without that rewrite, and the product tells them.
6. The text cannot be made ready: the product tells them, and the text is not lost.

### Choosing the rewriting

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) chooses once whether their [dictations](GLOSSARY.md#dictation) are [rewritten](../GLOSSARY.md#rewrite), and how, by their length. The choice holds for every dictation until they change it.

## How Deckle answers, a sample on two lines

### 1.3.3 They are never cut off

1. The [listening](GLOSSARY.md#listening) ends only on the [gesture](../GLOSSARY.md#gesture) that stops it: no length of time and no [silence](GLOSSARY.md#silence) ends it.
2. The [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) [transcribes](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe) the [voice](GLOSSARY.md#voice) [utterance](GLOSSARY.md#utterance) by utterance while the [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) speaks; a silence ends an utterance, and the listening goes on.
3. A [hesitation](GLOSSARY.md#hesitation) inside a sentence does not end an utterance: the product waits for a longer silence, between two sentences, before it ends one.

### 1.3.5 Nothing of what they said is lost

1. While the [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) speaks, the [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) keeps on the machine the sound of the [dictation](GLOSSARY.md#dictation) and the text [transcribed](../GLOSSARY.md#transcribe) so far, protected so that only the user can read them.
2. Keeping them never makes the [listening](GLOSSARY.md#listening) wait.
3. Once the text is given back, or the dictation is thrown away, the product removes what it kept.
4. When a dictation ended before its text was given back, the next time the product starts it transcribes again what it kept, takes the kept text where the sound gives none, and tells the user what came back.
