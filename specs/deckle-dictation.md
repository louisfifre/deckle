# Speak as long as you want, over everything, and get back a well-framed text

## Need

**Context of use.** A person thinks aloud, sometimes for more than an hour. They talk about what passes before their eyes, one subject then another, often while doing something else. They are at their computer, or elsewhere with their phone. What they say belongs to no application.

**User need.** The user needs to start the listening and stop attending to it, in order to get back everything they said in a well-framed text that they put wherever they want.

**Intended outcome.**

1. One gesture starts the listening from anywhere, and the same one stops it.
2. While they speak, nothing asks for their attention and nothing rushes them.
3. They are never cut off: a hesitation or a pause in the middle of a sentence closes nothing.
4. Only their voice counts: a rub or a noise around them does not pass for speech.
5. An hour holds up as well as two minutes, and nothing of what they said is lost.
6. They change subject, stop, resume: the change of subject is noticed by itself or on one gesture of theirs, and the text keeps these separations.
7. To find their thread again, their last words read at a glance, without hiding what they are looking at.
8. The final text says what they said, with their words.
9. The experience is the same on the computer and on the phone.

## Journey

| The user wants to | The product must |
| --- | --- |
| start speaking from wherever they are, without leaving what they do | start the dictation on one gesture, the same in every application, and leave them where they were |
| think aloud for as long as it takes, often doing something else | keep listening without asking for anything, without cutting them on a pause or a hesitation, and take only their voice |
| find their thread again | show their last words at a glance, out of the way of what they are looking at |
| change subject, pause, resume | notice the change of subject by itself or on one gesture of theirs, and keep these separations in the text |
| stop and use what they said | stop on the same gesture, and have the whole text ready, framed and in their words, for them to put where they want |
| choose once how their dictations are rewritten | apply that choice to every dictation, without asking again |

## Workflow

Each moment says what the user meets when it goes well, then what else can happen.

### Starting

When it goes well:

1. From wherever they are, one gesture starts a dictation.
2. Nothing is taken from what they were doing: they go on with it.
3. The first words they say after the gesture are in the text: they wait for no signal to speak.
4. The product shows them that it listens.

Otherwise:

- Nothing on the machine can transcribe speech: no dictation starts, and the product tells them what is missing and where to get it.
- No microphone answers: no dictation starts, and the product tells them so.

### Speaking

When it goes well:

1. The dictation goes on until they stop it, however long they are silent: nothing counts down, and they never make a sound to keep it going.
2. A hesitation, a stammer or a pause in the middle of a sentence never splits the sentence in the text.
3. Only their voice adds text: no other sound around them does.
4. Nothing the product shows presses them or asks for a gesture.
5. An hour goes as two minutes: the same gestures, nothing slows down.

Otherwise:

- The product hears no voice in the first seconds: it tells them once, and the dictation goes on.

### Finding the thread

When it goes well:

1. Their last words read at a glance, and the older text gives way to them.
2. What shows their words never hides what they are looking at: as they move toward it, what lies under it can be read and used.
3. What they read is the text they will get.
4. When the text is rewritten while they speak, what they are reading does not move.

Otherwise:

- A rewrite fails: they keep the text they were reading.

### Changing subject

When it goes well:

1. They change subject, pause and resume without leaving what they are doing.
2. The change of subject is noticed by itself, or marked by one gesture of theirs.
3. The text keeps each subject apart.

### Stopping

When it goes well:

1. The same gesture stops the dictation at once: the last words said before it are in the text, and nothing said after it is.
2. The whole text, rewritten when they chose it, is ready for them to paste where they want, and the product tells them so. Nothing is pasted for them.

Otherwise:

- Nothing was said: nothing is made ready, and what they had copied before stays ready to paste.
- Part of what they said was recorded but could not be transcribed: the rest is ready, and the product tells them a part is missing.
- A rewrite fails: they get their whole text, as said where the rewrite failed, and the product tells them.
- The text cannot be made ready to paste: the product tells them, and the text is not lost.

### Choosing the rewriting

1. The user chooses once whether their dictations are rewritten, and how, by their length. The choice holds for every dictation until they change it.

## Rules

1. Whatever stops a dictation, what was said in it is found again, and the product says so when it gives it back.
2. The text says what the user said, with their words: a rewrite may bring a notion said twice into one place, and never drops one.
3. What the product shows of the dictation is its real state: it says it listens only while it listens.
4. Everything the product tells the user reaches them with animations off as well.

## Surfaces

The product answers through these surfaces today; the moments above hold whatever they become.

- The gesture: the dictation shortcut, the same in every application, and Record in the tray.
- The state of the dictation and every message: the HUD.
- The last words and the text while they speak: the transcript panel, beside the HUD.
- The text ready to paste: the clipboard.
- The choice of rewriting: the Rewriting page, section Dictation.
