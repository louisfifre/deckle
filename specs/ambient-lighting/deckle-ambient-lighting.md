# Light the room with what your screen shows, vivid or realistic, fast or slow

> Not reviewed.

## Need

### Context of use

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) watches films and plays games on their computer, in a room lit by [lamps](GLOSSARY.md#lamp). A fast game and a slow film both pass on the same screen. The product is set up in more than one home, each with its own lamps.

### User need

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) needs the [lamps](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) of the room to follow what the screen shows, in order to have around them the light of what they watch or play.

### Intended outcome

1. The [lamps](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) of the room take the colours of what the screen shows.
2. Each lamp follows the [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) of the screen it is placed on.
3. Where the lamps stand in the room already gives each one its edge; the [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) changes it, or leaves a lamp out.
4. The light is [vivid](GLOSSARY.md#vivid) or [realistic](GLOSSARY.md#realistic), and it follows the screen fast or slow: two choices, each made apart from the other.
5. Without touching anything, the light is right, and the user can still tune it themselves.
6. They see how the screen is shared among their lamps without lighting them, and they see what a setting changes while they move it.

## Journey

| The user wants to | The product must |
| --- | --- |
| place their [lamps](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) | give each lamp the [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) its place in the room suggests, and let them change it or leave the lamp out |
| light the room from the screen | have each lamp follow its edge, and say why when it cannot |
| choose the light that fits what they watch or play | offer [vivid](GLOSSARY.md#vivid) or [realistic](GLOSSARY.md#realistic), and fast or slow, as two choices apart |
| tune the light themselves | let them change it beyond these choices, and show what each change does while they make it |
| see how the screen is shared | show it without lighting a lamp, with only the edges their lamps use |

## Rules

1. [What the product shows is its real state.](../deckle.rules.md)
2. [Everything the product tells the user reaches them with animations off as well.](../deckle.rules.md)
3. [Everything the product tells the user reaches them with colours off as well.](../deckle.rules.md)
4. A [lamp](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) goes dark only when its [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) of the screen is black.

## Workflow

### Placing the lamps

When it goes well:

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) connects the [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) to their [lamps](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) once.
2. Each lamp starts on the [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) of the screen that its place in the room gives it.
3. The user puts a lamp on another edge, or leaves it out.

### Lighting the room

When it goes well:

1. Once started, each [lamp](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) follows its [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) of the screen, whatever passes on it.

Otherwise:

2. The [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) cannot drive the lamps: no lamp changes, and the product says why and what to do.

### Choosing the light

When it goes well:

1. The [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) chooses a [vivid](GLOSSARY.md#vivid) or a [realistic](GLOSSARY.md#realistic) light, and chooses whether it follows the screen fast or slow. Each choice holds whatever the other is.
2. Without any choice of theirs, the light is right.
3. They tune the light further themselves, and see what each change does while they make it.

### Seeing how the screen is shared

When it goes well:

1. Without lighting a [lamp](GLOSSARY.md#lamp), the [user](../GLOSSARY.md#user) sees the screen shared among the [edges](GLOSSARY.md#edge), and only the edges their lamps use.

## How Deckle answers, a sample on two lines

### 1.3.1 The colours of what the screen shows

1. The [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) looks at the screen many times a second, and takes for each [edge](GLOSSARY.md#edge) the colour of the band of the picture along it.
2. It gives each [lamp](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) the colour of its edge, and sends nothing to a lamp left out.
3. A lamp goes dark only when the band of its edge is black.

### 1.3.4 Fast or slow

1. Fast lets each [lamp](GLOSSARY.md#lamp) follow a change of the scene at once, as a game asks; slow lets the colour settle over a moment, as a film asks.
2. The [product](../GLOSSARY.md#product) holds back a change too small to be seen, so that a lamp does not flicker, and lets a large one through.
3. [Vivid](GLOSSARY.md#vivid) makes the colours stronger than the screen gives them; [realistic](GLOSSARY.md#realistic) keeps them as the screen gives them.
